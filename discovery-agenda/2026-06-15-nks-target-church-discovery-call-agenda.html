Part A

What We Have Established

The working target profile and playbook, from the June 15 and June 16 calls. Treat it as settled unless NKS revises it.

Three ways a church engages: Rebrand, Retrofit, and Future

Every opportunity falls into one of three scenarios, which Frank summarized as existing, new, and future:

  • Rebrand (existing school). The church already runs a licensed day school; NKS assumes daily operations and rebrands it. Lowest cost (around $50,000) and fastest, as quick as 90 days. We avoid the word takeover, which carries negative connotations in the church world.
  • Retrofit (has space, no school). NKS renovates underused weekday space into a licensed, NKS-branded preschool. Higher cost and longer, closer to nine months.
  • Future (building campaign). The church is designing or fundraising a new building; NKS speaks into the architect's plans so a licensed, professionally managed preschool is built in from the start.

The ideal church partner, three archetypes

Frank, Mike, and Jenn converged on three profiles worth pursuing:

  • The network church. A large multi-site church in a major US city that overlaps a top childcare market. One church family can host five or more campuses across the city, a spiritual-impact multiplier and a built-in network.
  • The standalone flagship. A great church in a great market that can stand on its own. With the right church, market, and director, a single campus is a flagship.
  • The influential church. A church whose name and reach transcend geography. Influence is not just seats: Spanish River has planted 800-plus churches from Boca Raton. Landing one of these gives instant credibility and can open a whole network.

Frank's defining nuance: NKS wants a partner, not just another program of the church. The two gating questions are simply, do you have the space, and are you willing to embrace us as a partner.

Church size is now a qualifying factor

Average weekly attendance is a real screen. The sweet spot is 1,000 to about 3,500, with at least 1,000 as the working floor (an exceptional 800 can still work). Frank's logic, reinforced by advice from Justin Forman of Faith Driven Entrepreneur and RightNow Media, is that 1,500 to 3,500 churches genuinely need NKS, while much larger churches take far more time and tend to conclude they can self-operate. A church of a thousand tells you a lot: it likely has 8 to 10 classrooms, can afford the build-out, is community and evangelistically focused, and knows how to grow, scale, and lead. That makes a good partner.

Capacity and facility floor

The working student-capacity floor is 100 plus, with 125 as the standardized model. On the building, think five and five: roughly 5,000 square feet of interior classroom space and 5,000 square feet of adjacent outdoor playground. That is about 8 to 10 classrooms; the minimum individual classroom is around 550 square feet, since smaller rooms force wall removals to combine them. The playground must sit immediately adjacent to the preschool classrooms. Early in a conversation, ask the pastor for a floor plan and classroom sizes.

Markets and geography

Target the top childcare markets. Jenn flagged Texas (Dallas, Houston, Austin) as the anchor, where she has opened schools, and Frank followed up by email with the specific suburban pockets NKS wants to focus on first:

  • Dallas: Frisco, Plano, McKinney, Prosper, Allen, Southlake, and Flower Mound.
  • Houston: Katy, The Woodlands, Sugar Land, Cypress, and Pearland.
  • Austin: Cedar Park, Round Rock, Leander, Georgetown, Lakeway, and Bee Cave.

Frank notes these are the initial focus and that similarly positioned pockets elsewhere are fair game. Brian's strongest relationship bases add Atlanta, Charlotte, Phoenix, and Birmingham. Frank's guidance: focus on low-hanging fruit and high-influence relationships in top markets, and it does not have to be Texas, it is wherever Brian can pick up the phone today and get a church. Clustering five to seven in a metro is ideal (it justifies an area director), but a standalone flagship works too. A useful market signal: national franchise preschools nearby (Primrose, Goddard) mean abundant demand and a healthy neighborhood. California is not off the table; the hurdle is regulation more than cost, and NKS can secure separate funding and treat Orange County and similar areas as their own market.

Denominations and what is off-limits

NKS does not draw theological lines: if a church is under the banner of Jesus and will teach the gospel-centric curriculum without diverting it, NKS is open, and churches can review the curriculum and statement of faith. Two practical limits for now: NKS is not targeting Catholic dioceses or Catholic churches yet (not a theological objection, simply that there are more than enough Protestant and evangelical churches first; a diocese strategy may come later), and a church that requires enrollment families to believe exactly what the church believes is a poor fit, because it closes off reaching the broader community. AUDIA Consulting's filter holds: Great Commission-oriented churches across orthodox Christianity, primarily Evangelical, extending into mainline denominations with strong leadership.

Golden example one: the Rebrand model, First Baptist Church of Delray Beach

Our current golden example for the Rebrand model. The host church is First Baptist Church of Delray Beach (fbcdelray.com), a Southern Baptist church and a cornerstone of southern Palm Beach County for over seventy years, with a long-running school. The pastor, a former businessman and the local SBC leader, called Frank on a referral and said he no longer wanted to run the school. It was plug and play: zoned, operational, and licensed, with B-plus facilities. Frank's decisive question was whether he had authority to make leadership changes, and the pastor said yes. Because the school was already licensed, NKS got in for about $20,000.

What made it work is one name: Enma Vicente, Director of Neighborhood Kids at Delray, the highest-performing, lowest-turnover director in the network, with a waiting list. The takeaway: the director is vital, but Delray works because every factor aligned at once, a strong director, an evangelistic pastor who embraced the partnership, a make-ready licensed facility, and a supportive market. It is small (about 90 to 93 capacity, below the 100-plus target) yet thrives for exactly that reason.

Golden example two: the Retrofit model, Spanish River Church (in progress)

Our live Retrofit example is Spanish River Church in Boca Raton, one of the most influential churches in America by virtue of planting more than 800 churches. It runs Spanish River Christian Academy (a K-12 school since the 1980s) but had no early-childhood program, and asked NKS to add one by retrofitting the educational building. NKS is quoting roughly $500,000 for the build-out given the older building (wall removals, extensive plumbing, exterior playground egress, and a new playground), targeting a 162-child campus. It is at the signed-LOI and preview stage, so it is a working framework rather than a proven, operating golden example yet, and NKS is referencing it with a Dallas family office as a model deal.

The economics: a roughly 50/50 partnership

The church funds the license-ready build-out and furnishings. For a new school the church investment averages $250,000 to $300,000, scaling with size: about $250K at 125 students, $300K at 150 to 175, and $350K to $400K at 200 (add roughly $50K per capacity tier, since a bigger school needs more sinks and space). An existing school (Rebrand) can be as little as $50,000. The single biggest line is the playground, $125,000 to $150,000 for a top-notch build that signals a family church. The crucial selling point: NKS invests roughly the same amount as the church, plus it carries staffing and payroll until breakeven, so it is genuinely a 50/50 partnership until the school cash-flows. NKS provides a pro forma showing the church recaptures its investment within about 24 months, leaving it net positive for eight of a ten-year term.

The church's return: a shared-use agreement on top-line revenue

The church receives a percentage of gross (top-line) revenue, deliberately not profit, so both sides stay focused on filling seats while NKS owns profitability. The baseline is 8 percent. On a ten-year deal NKS can step it up, 8 percent, then 9, then 10 (the Spanish River LOI uses 9 percent in years 1 to 3 and 10 percent in years 4 to 8 on a 3+5 term), and Brian and the team aligned that any step-up should be an incentive tied to the church helping reach 90-plus percent enrollment, never automatic. In a weaker market, hold the line at 8 percent. For scale: at 125 students (about $2M revenue) 8 percent is roughly $160,000 a year; a 100-student campus is closer to $14,000 a month; 300 students is about $4.5M in revenue. In the contract this is called a shared-use or shared-space agreement (not revenue or lease) to keep churches clear of unrelated-business-income tax concerns, though in conversation Brian can plainly call it a revenue share.

Leadership is the multiplier, and the director is recruitable

The center director is vital, a believer who is also a strong leader, and more than almost anything else everything rises and falls on leadership, but a campus does not begin and end with the director: it needs the director, evangelistic church leadership, a make-ready facility, and a supportive market together. On staffing a campus, this is not a constraint in the target markets: the population density supports it, and NKS also retains a national recruiter who works only in the education space and has a heart for God. When NKS rebrands an existing school, the priority is to keep as much existing staff as possible for parent continuity, with everyone qualified through a quick interview to confirm they are on mission.

What kills a deal, the four mistakes

  • Underestimating the church's cost to get ready. One church was quoted around $500,000; surprises (a county-required turn lane, a bad land-use firm) pushed it to 3x. NKS now gives a sober, realistic estimate for the church to bid out.
  • Choosing the wrong church. You cannot exit the contract, and a bad campus becomes a bad reference.
  • Relationship over fit. Excitement and a great pastor connection, but the market will not fill the rooms or the building cannot be licensed without unfunded capital. Bring NKS a fit, not just a relationship.
  • A partner dishonoring the brand. For AUDIA Consulting the biggest risk is overpromising or anything that damages NKS's reputation. Protect the relationship and do not overspeak.

Brian's Lionheart experience confirmed all of this. The frequent obstructionists were the existing school's teachers (for example a Mother's Day Out program where teachers wanted only three days), not the pastoral team.

Partnership mindset and pastor reorientation

Pastors instinctively think about cash, disruption, and how turnkey it is. The job is to reorient them to the Great Commission and position NKS as a partner that helps grow the church. NKS is a ministry and extension of the church, not just a preschool, and the branding honors the host church: it can be co-branded, for example "Connect Kids, powered by Neighborhood Kids," rather than stamping NKS everywhere. The frame Brian uses is win-win, same team, us-with-you rather than us-versus-them. The ideal is an on-fire church that wraps its arms around the school, but a busy, non-obstructionist church that lets NKS run the school and prays with the kids can still be a great campus.

The internal champion model

NKS stipends the church's existing kids or children's director $500 to $1,000 a month to serve as liaison. That person is already in the building and motivated, so it honors their time, builds a communication bridge, and lets NKS learn the church's DNA. This replaces the older Lionheart model of a brand-new church-hired liaison, which drifted into school operations rather than strategic outreach.

Accreditation: NKS is pursuing ACSI

NKS is pursuing accreditation with the Association of Christian Schools International (ACSI), the leading Christian school accreditor (founded 1978, roughly 25,000 schools across 108 countries, with an early-education arm for birth through kindergarten). It is a credibility asset to raise with pastors: a recognized Christ-centered standard, stronger community standing, and possible grant eligibility. One practical implication Frank noted: the space must look like a school, not a sterile multi-million-dollar build. Represent accreditation accurately as in process, never as already accredited.

The deal flow and the perfect handoff

The document path is Ministry Partnership Preview, then Letter of Intent, then Ministry Affiliation Agreement (MAA). The non-binding LOI triggers NKS to begin feasibility, modeling, licensing prep, and director recruitment at no cost to the church; it is valid 18 months with a 60-day path to the MAA. Sometimes NKS skips the LOI and goes straight to the MAA to avoid a second document for the church to mull over.

The relationship handoff routes by influence: a lead or executive pastor goes to Frank first (he texts them, brings Jenn in as it warms); a co-pastor or lead-team member, often a husband-and-wife team, goes to a group text with Frank and Jenn so everyone is comfortable. Brian sends an intro plus the NKS value-proposition video; NKS responds the same day. A flawless handoff means Brian stays on the thread, especially when the relationship is warm. Jenn then requests floor plans and runs a quick viability study, and the first real meeting is Frank and Jenn, sometimes Mike. NKS has engaged the consulting group Catapult (Todd Milby) on messaging and sales process, is building a one-pager and brand assets for Brian, and plans to hire a VP of Sales once funded, with AUDIA Consulting helping vet and train that person under a separate engagement.

Pipeline capacity, cadence, and where to focus

NKS can absorb about 70 strong warm introductions over the summer (July through September) and could handle 100. Frank's conservative funnel: 70 introductions, roughly half clear the demographic, mission, and facility screens, about a third of those get LOIs (around 25), and a fifth to a third close, which is about 15 schools. The plan is to open 3 in 2026, 10 in 2027, and a couple in 2028 from building-campaign churches. NKS runs a Monday development meeting and wants dozens of churches in the pipeline at varying stages. The strategy is to feed the pipeline with low-hanging fruit, churches with real pain and evangelistic drive that move fast, while putting the large aircraft-carrier churches in a multi-year queue and checking in periodically. Winning the quick, lower-investment churches also builds brand momentum.
