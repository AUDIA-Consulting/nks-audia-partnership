Target Church Discovery Call

Defining the Golden Example for the NKS & AUDIA Consulting Church Referral Partnership

A working session between Neighborhood Kids Schools and AUDIA Consulting

Purpose of This Call

Under our Strategic Church Referral Partnership, AUDIA Consulting sources, maps, and warmly introduces missionally aligned churches to NKS. The quality of those introductions depends entirely on how precisely we understand the target. The goal of this call is for NKS leadership to define the golden example: a clear, shared picture of the ideal partner church, why that profile wins, and exactly what disqualifies a church before AUDIA Consulting ever spends a relationship on it.

This agenda is built as a full discovery conversation. The questions move from the big picture of who we are looking for, into the specific facility, demographic, theological, financial, and leadership requirements, then into the operational realities that shape which churches are worth pursuing first. The last section is reserved for context NKS wants AUDIA Consulting to carry, and for the most important question of all: what should we have asked that we did not.

1.Framing and Shared Objectives

The big-picture target All

Before we get into criteria, I want to hear it in your own words.

  1. If you could describe the single ideal church partner in a sentence or two, what does that church look like?
  2. When you picture the next 70 churches you want signed, what do the strongest ones have in common?
  3. What does a win look like to NKS twelve months after a school opens, and what does it look like at the five and ten year mark?
  4. What is the biggest mistake an outside partner like AUDIA Consulting could make when deciding which churches to bring you?
2.The Golden Example, Decoded
Frank has pointed to Delray Beach Baptist Church as an outstanding example, a smaller program NKS took over a few years ago that is now the best performing school, anchored by an exceptional Center Director. I want to take that example apart so we can recognize the next one early.

What made Delray Beach work All

  1. Walk me through the Delray Beach story from first contact to where it is today. What did the church look like before NKS, and what changed?
  2. Of everything that contributed to that success, what were the two or three factors that mattered most, and which of those can we screen for in advance?
  3. How much of that success was the church itself, how much was the market, and how much was the Center Director? If you had to weight them, how would you?
  4. What was true about Delray Beach at the start that did not look obvious, but turned out to be a major advantage?
  5. If we found ten more churches exactly like Delray Beach tomorrow, would you take all ten? If not, why not?
What I am listening for: the repeatable pattern behind the golden example, so I can spot it in a church I have a relationship with rather than only recognizing it after the fact.

The other side of the ledger Jenn Mike

  1. What is the clearest example of a church that looked good on paper but did not work, and what was the root cause?
  2. Are there churches you have walked away from, and what was the deciding factor?
  3. When a school underperforms, is it more often a church problem, a market problem, a director problem, or an execution problem?
3.Facility and Physical Requirements
Baseline figures from our alignment work: roughly 1,000 plus average weekly attendance, 8 to 10 dedicated classrooms, and at least 5,000 square feet of outdoor playground space. I want to confirm, sharpen, and understand the flexibility around each.

The non-negotiables Mike Jenn

  1. Of the facility requirements, which are truly hard lines and which are preferences we could flex on for the right church?
  2. What is the realistic minimum classroom count for a viable school, and what is the count where the economics really start to work?
  3. How important is dedicated versus shared space? Can classrooms or the playground be shared with the church's weekend or midweek ministries, or do they need to be ours full time?
  4. When Jenn walks a site, what are the first things she looks at, and what are the silent deal killers most people would miss?
  5. What facility features turn an acceptable site into an exceptional one, the things that make a school easy to fill and easy to run?
  6. How do parking, drop-off flow, separate entrances, and bathroom or sink locations factor into whether a building works?
4.Market, Demographics, and Density
Established direction: average local household income of $100,000 plus, Texas as an anchor market at roughly 40 percent of the US childcare market, no states off the table, and a goal of cluster density of around five sustainable churches within a 5 to 10 mile radius. Frank also intends to share the top 150 US childcare markets.

What makes a market worth entering Mike Frank

  1. Beyond household income, what demographic signals tell you a market will fill a school: young families, birth rates, daytime workforce, competing childcare supply, waitlists?
  2. How do you think about a strong church in a weak market versus an average church in a strong market? Which way do you lean and why?
  3. Can you walk me through the cluster density logic? Is a single excellent church in a standalone market a yes, a no, or a maybe?
  4. When the top 150 markets list comes over, how should AUDIA Consulting weight that list against the strength of an actual relationship I can open a door with?
  5. Are there markets that are saturated, regulated, or otherwise difficult enough that I should deprioritize them even with a great church?
  6. How does local childcare licensing or state regulation change how attractive a market is to you?
5.Theological and Missional Alignment
Working profile: Protestant evangelical churches that emphasize community outreach, missional growth, or a multi-site footprint, with the model integrating across 100 plus denominations. The church must genuinely prioritize Christian education and not treat the school as an afterthought or a quick cash infusion.

Mission fit and the right reasons Frank

  1. How do you define genuine missional alignment, and how do you tell the difference between a church that wants ministry impact and one that mostly wants revenue or building utilization?
  2. Are there denominations, theological positions, or church cultures that are an especially strong fit, and any that are a poor fit or off limits?
  3. You have described ultra-large churches where the academy becomes an overlooked sub-program as a poor fit. Where is the upper boundary, and how do I recognize it?
  4. What questions should I be asking a pastor early to test whether their heart for Christian education is real and durable, not just enthusiasm in the first meeting?
  5. How important is the church's own children's or family ministry today as a signal of fit?
6.Leadership and Relational Fit

The pastor and the decision Frank Jenn

  1. Who actually makes this decision at a church: the lead pastor, an executive pastor, an elder board, a school board? Who do I most need on side?
  2. What kind of pastor or leadership team makes the best long-term partner, in terms of temperament, decisiveness, and how they run their church?
  3. What leadership red flags should make me cautious even when everything else looks good?
  4. The alignment work referenced an internal champion model, designating a church staff member as a paid liaison. What does the ideal internal champion look like, and how early should I be identifying that person?
  5. How stable does church leadership need to be? Does a pending pastoral transition or a young, unproven church change whether I should bring it to you?
7.Financial Capacity and Church Economics
Established expectation: the church can fund roughly $100,000 to $200,000 in facility improvements, such as playground equipment, furniture, and classroom sinks, and recapture that investment within 12 to 18 months. NKS has financing capacity to deploy alongside this.

Can the church carry its side Mike

  1. How do I assess a church's financial capacity without being intrusive early in the relationship? What are tactful signals of capital health?
  2. Is the $100,000 to $200,000 improvement budget a firm requirement, or is there a path for a strong church that cannot fund all of it upfront?
  3. What does the recapture math look like in practice, so I can speak credibly to a pastor about how the investment pays back in 12 to 18 months?
  4. When does NKS bring its own capital to the table, and how should I frame that with a church so it is an asset in the conversation rather than a giveaway?
  5. Are there financial situations, debt load, declining giving, recent building campaigns, that should disqualify a church even if the mission fit is strong?
8.The Center Director Factor
The Delray Beach success was anchored by an exceptional Center Director, Enma, whose servant leadership drove low turnover, full enrollment, and high satisfaction. The alignment work flagged the ability to recruit a strong director as one of the best predictors of success.

Recruitability as a screening factor Jenn

  1. Should the recruitability of a great Center Director in a given market be a factor in whether I bring you a church at all? How heavily?
  2. What does a great Center Director look like, and what makes some markets much easier to staff than others?
  3. Can a strong church overcome a weak local labor market for directors and teachers, or is that close to a hard limit?
  4. Is there anything about a church or community that makes it easier or harder to attract the right director, for example proximity to talent, the church's own reputation, or compensation norms in the area?
9.Deal Dynamics, Disqualifiers, and Speed
Pipeline benchmarks from our alignment work: aim for a clear no within 30 days or a signed agreement within 120 days, with lead-to-launch running 90 days to 9 months depending on whether it is a merger of an existing preschool or a build-out.

What kills a deal, and what accelerates one Jenn Frank

  1. What are the most common reasons a promising church conversation falls apart, and which of those can I head off in the early relationship-mapping stage?
  2. What are the early objections you hear most from pastors, around facility disruption, team impact, control, or finances, and how do you want me to handle them upfront?
  3. Is there a meaningful difference in how I should target a merger candidate, a church with an existing preschool, versus a build-out candidate with no program today? Which do you want more of right now?
  4. What does an ideal church look like on the timeline, the kind that gets to a clean yes inside 120 days rather than dragging?
  5. Are there absolute disqualifiers, the things that mean do not even start the conversation, that I should keep in front of me?
10.Process, Roles, and What Happens After the Introduction

The handoff and the cadence All

  1. Once I make a warm introduction, what is the exact sequence on your side: who reaches out, how fast, and what does the first NKS conversation cover?
  2. At what point does Jenn get involved, and what does her accelerated feasibility study look like from the church's point of view?
  3. How can AUDIA Consulting best support after the introduction without getting in the way of your close? Where does my involvement add value and where should I step back?
  4. What information do you most want from me when I hand over a church, so your team can move fast and look prepared?
  5. How do you want to handle pre-contact vetting? Should I run candidates past Jenn before any outreach so we avoid spending relationships on weak-fit churches?
11.Priorities, Sequencing, and Where to Start

First moves Frank Mike

  1. If AUDIA Consulting could deliver you the perfect first three churches in the next 90 days, where would they be and what would they look like?
  2. Are there specific regions, markets, or church networks you most want me focused on first, given your current development capacity?
  3. Is there a profile you want me to actively avoid right now, even if it is a good church, because the timing or fit is wrong for this phase?
  4. How many strong introductions can your team realistically absorb at once, so I pace sourcing to match your capacity rather than flooding you?
12.Context and Open Floor

What else should I be carrying All

  1. What else do you think I need to know to represent NKS well and find the right churches, anything we have not touched on?
  2. Is there context about NKS, your story, your model, or your standards that helps me speak credibly and passionately to a pastor?
  3. How do you want AUDIA Consulting to talk about NKS when I am opening a door, the language, the framing, the proof points that resonate most with church leaders?
  4. Is there anything you do not want me to say or promise on your behalf in those early conversations?
The most important question: What have I not asked that I should have? If you were in my seat trying to find your next ten golden-example churches, what would you want to know that we have not covered today?
